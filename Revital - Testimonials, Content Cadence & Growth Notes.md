## 2. Content / Blog Cadence (using Marketing News as input)

**Cadence:** One post every two weeks. Sustainable for a small team, frequent enough that Google and repeat visitors notice, and matches roughly what the Marketing News feed will surface as "worth reacting to" in a given stretch.

**Four content pillars:**

1. **Trend reaction** — Take one headline surfaced in Marketing News and give a short, opinionated take on what it means for small/growing brands specifically. Low lift: the sourcing is already automated, this is just the write-up.
2. **Behind the scenes** — Process content from actual shoots/events/builds you're already doing. Doesn't require new work, just documenting work already happening (a phone photo + 200 words is enough).
3. **Client spotlights** — Short case-study-style posts once you have real outcomes to share (ties directly into the proof-point gap flagged earlier — this is where flagship case studies would live).
4. **Practical how-to** — Tactical tips aimed at "brands on the rise": e.g. how to prep for a first brand shoot, what to have ready before a live event, what actually goes into a visual identity package. Builds authority and targets people early in their search, before they're ready to book a call.

**Workflow:**
- Rotate ownership rather than assigning it to one person permanently — sustainable pace matters more than who writes it.
- Host as a Framer CMS blog collection so it lives on-domain (better for SEO than an external platform).
- Repost/link from the existing Instagram, Facebook, and LinkedIn accounts already in the site footer — no new channel needed, just reuse what's there.

**Starter topic list (mix of pillars, ready to assign):**
1. "3 marketing trends worth reacting to this month" (trend reaction)
2. "What actually happens on a Revital shoot day" (behind the scenes)
3. "How [client] used live streaming to extend a one-night event into a week of content" (client spotlight — once a client agrees to be featured with real numbers)
4. "5 things to have ready before your first brand shoot" (how-to)
5. "Brand strategy vs. visual identity: what's actually the difference, and which do you need first" (how-to, also doubles as pricing-page education)
6. "What we watch for before setting up a Google Ads account" (how-to / trend reaction hybrid)
7. "Inside a live event build: lighting and audio decisions nobody sees" (behind the scenes)
8. "Why 'brands on the rise' need a different playbook than established brands" (positioning piece — reinforces your own site copy)

## 3. Capacity & Bigger-Client Push (strategy note)

**Where things stand:** the hours-based capacity tracking just went live for production/creative roles, and per your own read there's no active bottleneck right now. That's useful information on its own — it means there's room before it becomes a constraint, which is the ideal time to make a deliberate choice about what fills it.

**The suggestion:** don't let that open capacity get absorbed by default into more of the same size/type of project. Point at least some of it at business development aimed at the "Enterprise" ($3,000+) campaign tier, which is currently underrepresented in both your booked work and your public case studies.

**A concrete loop to run this on:**
1. Let 2–4 weeks of real data accumulate in Hours & Time Log now that logging is actually happening.
2. Check Team Roster's new hours-based capacity bars to see which roles genuinely have room (Video Editor and Web Developer capacity will look different from Paid Media Lead's, for instance) — that tells you which kind of new business you're actually staffed to take on right now.
3. Pair that with 1–2 flagship case studies from clients willing to share real numbers (ties directly back to the testimonials/proof-point gap above) — that's usually the deciding factor for a prospect sizing up whether to book the $3,000+ tier instead of the $1,200 one.

This isn't a one-time task so much as a habit: the capacity data is now live and free to check, so it's worth glancing at before each new-business push rather than only when something feels overbooked.

## 4. Ad/Creative Research Tools ("board of ads and what's performing")

Came up while looking into Motion (motionapp.com) — worth keeping as a reference next time competitor/trend ad research comes up. Cheapest to most built-out:

- **Meta Ad Library** (ads.meta.com) — official, completely free. Search any advertiser or keyword, see every ad they're currently running on Facebook/Instagram. No save/board feature and no cross-brand trend view — one advertiser at a time.
- **Motion's Creative Research** — free, just launched as part of motionapp.com (the ad-analytics Motion, not the calendar app usemotion.com). Track competitors on paid social, browse curated collections of high-performing ads across industries, save Meta/TikTok ads to your own swipe file. This is the closest match to "a board of ads and what's performing."
- **Foreplay** — paid, built for teams: shared boards, annotations, and turning saved ads directly into creative briefs. Worth it only if/when the team wants to collaborate on the same board rather than one person browsing.

**Starting point:** Motion's Creative Research — it's free and already tied to the Motion product from the earlier research. Move to Foreplay only if the team outgrows a single-user swipe file.
